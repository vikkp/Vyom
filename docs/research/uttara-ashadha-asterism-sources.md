# Uttara Ashadha: asterism research

## Decision

A two-star line -- the existing yogatara Nunki (σ Sagittarii) joined by
Ascella (ζ Sagittarii). New `UTTARA_ASHADHA_EXTRA_STARS` group, sharing
`nodeId: "uttara-ashadha"` with the existing yogatara.

## Sourcing

- **Stars, and a discrepancy worth flagging**: most pop-astrology sources
  describe Uttara Ashadha as "a group of 4 bright stars... Sigma, Tau,
  Phi and Zeta Sagittarii," while a cited academic source states plainly
  that "Uttarā Āṣāḍhā corresponds to σ, ζ Sagittarii" -- just the two
  brightest. Went with the narrower, academically-sourced two-star
  pairing (matching this batch's other "Uttara" nakshatras, Uttara
  Phalguni and the earlier Uttara Bhadrapada, which are both clean
  two-star lines) rather than the broader four-star folk description,
  similar to the Eta-vs-Mu Hydrae judgment call made for Ashlesha
  earlier -- narrower, better-corroborated citation preferred over a
  looser popular one. One source's search summary also reversed which
  Bayer letter maps to which common name (claiming Sigma=Ascella,
  Zeta=Nunki) -- double-checked against the existing catalog entry
  (`id: "nunki"`, coordinates matching Sigma Sagittarii exactly) and
  against Ascella's own Wikipedia page (Zeta Sagittarii) to confirm the
  standard, correct pairing: Nunki = Sigma, Ascella = Zeta.
- **Symbol**: an elephant tusk and a small bed/cot -- rest and repose
  after victory, foundational support. The tusk symbol echoes Purva
  Ashadha's (the preceding nakshatra) tusk symbol, a deliberate paired
  motif in the tradition.
- **Ruling deity**: the Vishvadevas, the ten universal gods governing
  collective positive qualities and righteous action.
