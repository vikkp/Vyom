# Ashlesha: asterism research

## Decision

A closed six-star loop -- the existing yogatara Epsilon Hydrae joined by
Delta, Eta, Rho, Sigma, and Zeta Hydrae, the compact "ring of stars"
forming the head of Hydra. New `ASHLESHA_EXTRA_STARS` group, all sharing
`nodeId: "ashlesha"` with the existing yogatara.

## Sourcing

- **Stars**: multiple sources describe Ashlesha as "a ring of stars in the
  constellation of Hydra, known in modern astronomy as Epsilon Hydrae,
  Delta Hydrae, [[Eta or Mu]] Hydrae, Rho Hydrae, Sigma Hydrae & Zeta
  Hydrae" -- six stars total, matching the yogatara plus five companions.
  ([Astrology-Prophets](https://www.astrology-prophets.com/Nakshatras/ashlesha-nakshatra.php),
  cross-checked against a second aggregated summary citing the same core
  group)
- **Eta vs. Mu Hydrae discrepancy**: one aggregator source lists "Mu
  Hydrae" as the sixth star instead of "Eta Hydrae." Checked both
  candidates' actual coordinates: Mu Hydrae sits at roughly RA 10.9h, Dec
  -16.8° -- in Hydra's body/tail, nowhere near the compact head cluster
  the other five stars form (RA 8.6-8.9h, Dec 3-6°). Eta Hydrae, by
  contrast, sits at RA 8.72h, Dec 3.4°, squarely inside that same cluster.
  [Wikipedia's own star pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eta_Hydrae)
  additionally note that Delta, Sigma, Rho, Zeta, **and Eta** Hydrae
  together were historically catalogued as Ulug Beg's "Min al Az'al"
  ("Belonging to the Uninhabited Spot") -- an independently documented
  historical asterism grouping that corroborates Eta over Mu. Went with
  Eta Hydrae as the sixth star; Mu Hydrae's citation looks like an
  aggregator error rather than a genuine alternate tradition.
- **Symbol**: a coiled serpent, tied to Kundalini/hidden power imagery --
  fitting the compact, ring-like real shape these six stars actually form.
  ([Rudraksha Ratna](https://www.rudraksha-ratna.com/articles/ashlesha-nakshatra))
- **Ruling deity**: the Nagas, serpent deities associated with
  transformation, concealment, and mystic power. Ruled by Mercury (Budha).
  ([Astropush](https://astropush.com/blog/ashlesha-nakshatra-guide))

| Rho Hydrae | 8.8072 | 5.8378 | 4.34 | [Wikipedia: Rho Hydrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho_Hydrae) |
| Sigma Hydrae | 8.6460 | 3.3414 | 4.48 | [Wikipedia: Sigma Hydrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Hydrae) |
| Zeta Hydrae | 8.9232 | 5.9456 | 3.10 | [Wikipedia: Zeta Hydrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeta_Hydrae) |

Plotting RA as x / Dec as y: Sigma and Eta form a lower pair (Dec ~3.3-3.4)
while Delta, Epsilon, Rho, and Zeta form an upper pair-of-pairs (Dec
~5.7-6.4) -- two clusters connected diagonally, which reads naturally as a
coil/hook rather than a straight line. Traversal path (closed loop):
Delta Hydrae -> Sigma Hydrae -> Eta Hydrae -> Epsilon Hydrae -> Zeta
Hydrae -> Rho Hydrae -> Delta Hydrae. This ordering was checked
specifically to avoid line segments passing close to un-connected stars
(an earlier candidate ordering had the closing segment pass almost
exactly through Rho Hydrae's position; swapping Zeta and Rho's order in
the loop fixed it).
