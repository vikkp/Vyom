## Star identification

Magha's existing yogatara is Regulus (α Leonis) — "securely identified across all historical sources... the main star (yogatara)." The surrounding nakshatra-asterism is documented as five further stars:

- "The nakshatra-asterism surrounding [Regulus] consists of five fainter stars: γ, η, ο, 31 and ρ Leonis." [All Skies Encyclopaedia: Maghā](https://xing.fmi.uni-jena.de/mediawiki/index.php/Magha) — a peer-referenced historical-astronomy wiki (Universität Jena / Austrian Academy of Sciences), citing the Brihat Samhita's yogatara tradition used throughout this project.

Coordinates (all cross-checked against Wikipedia/TheSkyLive):

- **Algieba (γ¹ Leonis)** — RA 10h19m58.351s (10.3329h), Dec +19°50′29.35″ (19.8415°), mag 2.08 (combined naked-eye pair).
- **Eta Leonis (Al Jabhah)** — RA 10h07m19.952s (10.1222h), Dec +16°45′45.59″ (16.7627°), mag 3.486.
- **Omicron Leonis (Subra)** — RA 09h41m09.032s (9.6858h), Dec +09°53′32.31″ (9.8923°), mag 3.52.
- **31 Leonis** — RA 10h07m54.270s (10.1317h), Dec +09°59′51.03″ (9.9975°), mag 4.39.
- **Rho Leonis** — RA 10h32m48.672s (10.5469h), Dec +09°18′23.71″ (9.3066°), mag ~3.85 (slightly variable, 3.83–3.90).

All six (Regulus + these five) share `nodeId: "magha"`.

## Shape

This isn't the familiar Western "Sickle of Leo" (which uses ζ/μ/ε Leonis instead) — it's a distinct, Hindu-tradition-specific asterism. Plotting the six stars by position: Omicron, 31, and Rho Leonis cluster at low declination (~9–10°) forming a shallow base, while Regulus, Eta, and Algieba rise north from there (12° → 17° → 20°) in a near-vertical line. Traced as a single path following that layout — Rho → 31 Leonis → Omicron → Regulus → Eta → Algieba — the shape reads as a base rising into a tall back, which fits Magha's traditional symbol (a royal throne/palanquin, for the nakshatra of ancestral/royal authority) better than a literal transcription would suggest by coincidence of the real star positions.
